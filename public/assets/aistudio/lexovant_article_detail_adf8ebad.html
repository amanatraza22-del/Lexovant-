Case Matter

Criminal Appeal No. 2891 of 2024 (Arising out of SLP Crl.)

Statute Interplay

Section 17A, PC Act (1988)

Amended 2018; Reconciled with Section 7 bribe acceptance protocols.

In a landmark ruling reinforcing procedural boundaries, the Supreme Court of India clarified the exact jurisdictional scope of Section 17A of the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (as inserted by the 2018 Amendment). The court tackled an enduring constitutional tension: does a public official caught red-handed accepting illegal gratification enjoy statutory immunity requiring prior approval before preliminary inquiry?

The Apex Court firmly reiterated that Section 17A was designed strictly as a defensive bastion for decision-makers against frivolous or motivated complaints, never as an imperviable cloak for explicit criminal demands or disproportionate asset accumulation.

Key Statutory Takeaways

check
Exclusion of Red-Handed Trap Cases

Prior sanctions or approvals are explicitly unnecessary where an arrest occurs during a lawful police trap or simultaneous demand and acceptance of graft under Section 7.

check
Protected Official Deliberations

Section 17A shields bona fide administrative files, notes, recommendations, and policy discretion exercised within standard departmental capacity.

check
Time-Bound Sanction Protocol

Competent authorities must accord or reject permission within three months, extendable by one extra month with reasoned written justification.

format_quote
Judicial Dictum

"The protection under Section 17A is restricted to decisions taken or recommendations made in discharge of official functions. The demand or acceptance of illegal gratification can under no circumstance be categorized as part of an official recommendation."

— Hon’ble Justice Sanjiv Khanna, Supreme Court of India

Practical Compliance Checklist

3 Essential Steps
1. What constitutes a valid Section 17A defense? expand_more
To invoke Section 17A, the accused must prove that the subject inquiry relates directly to a documented administrative recommendation or policy verdict made in good faith during the discharge of standard public duties.
2. Can investigative agencies initiate inquiry without sanction? expand_more
Only in immediate apprehension scenarios (traps) or private extortion complaints detached from official policy deliberations. For all systemic internal administrative allegations, prior written approval from the designated appointing officer remains mandatory.
3. Remit for corporate contractors and third-party vendors expand_more
Corporate entities facing inquiries under Section 8 (bribing of a public servant) cannot claim the protective cloak of 17A, as the protection is personalized strictly to public personnel.
